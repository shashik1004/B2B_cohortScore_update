1Executive summary

The B2B score asks one question about each correspondent and co-issue client: do its loans pay off faster, and get recaptured less, than look-alike loans from other lenders? The new model places every loan in a fixed peer cohort when the loan is priced. It then compares the client's actual prepayment speed with the actual speed of all peer loans in those same cohorts.

WHAT IS NEW

  • The benchmark is what peer loans actually did, not a forecast. The older score divided a client's CPR by the Situs model's projected CPR. That forecast missed by different amounts for different loan types (VA: 8.6% projected vs 22.4% actual among peers), which distorted client rankings.
  • Each loan's peer group is set once. The cohort is set from the loan's attributes at lock (Correspondent) or funding (Co-Issue) and never changes.
  • Every score can be traced back. The cohort-level detail behind each client's CPRScore is stored in Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_CPRScore_Audit, so anyone can see what drove a score and re-derive it (Section 8).

2What the B2B score is & how it is used

The B2B score measures how a client's loans behave after funding compared with similar loans from other clients. It looks at two behaviors: how quickly the loans prepay, and how much balance Onity recaptures when they pay off.

OutputQuestion it answersHow to read it
CPRScoreDo this client's loans prepay faster or slower than peer loans in the same cohorts?Lower is better. 1.00 = same as peers; 0.72 = 28% slower; 1.33 = 33% faster.
RecapScoreWhen this client's loans pay off, does Onity recapture more or less balance than the benchmark?Higher is better. 1.00 = at benchmark.
TotalScoreTaking both together, how does this client compare?Higher is better. RecapScore ÷ CPRScore.
F / N / U tagWhere does the client stand against the whole book?F Favorable ≥ 1.25 × portfolio · N Neutral · U Unfavorable < 0.75 × portfolio

How it is used

  • B2B pricing input. The client's TotalScore and F / N / U tag are the model's outputs for B2B pricing.
  • Breakdowns. The same scores are produced by state, FICO, LTV, DTI, note rate, product, average loan balance, channel and client, plus Client × State and Client × State × Product views. These show where a client is fast or slow.
  • Explaining a score. The audit table shows, cohort by cohort, how each client's loans compared with their peers (Section 8.2).
In one sentence
A client scores well only when its loans prepay slower than loans like its own, or when it recaptures more. The comparison is always against what peer loans actually did in the same months.

3How the score is built

1
Assign each loan to a cohort
Once, at lock or funding, from five loan features.
2
Measure the client's actual CPR
How fast the client's loan balances ran off during the score window, annualized.
3
Build the peer benchmark
Actual CPR of all loans, from all clients, in the cohorts where the client has loans.
4
CPRScore = actual ÷ benchmark
Below 1 means slower than peers, which is good.
5
TotalScore = RecapScore ÷ CPRScore
Then tag F / N / U against the portfolio score.
The prepayment model is used only to define the peers
When the cohorts were designed, projected prepayment speeds were used to find which loan features drive prepayment and where to draw band boundaries. The score itself never uses a projection. The benchmark is always the observed run-off of peer loans over the same months.

5Stage 1 — Construct peer cohorts

Goal: group hundreds of thousands of loans into a manageable, explainable set of peer groups. Loans in a group should prepay at about the same speed, and each group should hold enough loans to be reliable.

5.1 Data used

  • Two monthly loan snapshots (June 30 and July 31, 2026), pooled together: about 1.21 million loan observations covering 626,132 distinct active loans.
  • Included: active GSE and GNMA loans. Excluded: PLS loans, Ocwen / Ocwen REO loans and loans with an unknown seller.
  • Each loan's rate spread was measured against the market rate in effect for its own snapshot month.

5.2 Which loan features matter

Candidate features were tested for how strongly, and how consistently in both months, they relate to prepayment speed. A shallow decision tree was used to confirm the ranking.

FeatureLink to prepayment speedUsed to define cohorts?
Rate spread (note rate − market rate)Very strong. By far the main driver: loans that are "in the money" to refinance prepay much faster.Yes
Remaining termModerate. Improved accuracy on loans held out of the build.Yes
Loan balanceModerate. Larger loans refinance faster when in the money.Yes
Loan type (Conventional / FHA / VA / Other)Small on its own, but loan types behave differently.Yes
Original FICOWeak statistically. Kept as a business requirement.Yes, fixed split at 750
Original LTV, ARM flag, investor, loan ageWeak, or excluded by design.No

5.3 Bands and how they are combined

FeatureBandsCan bands be combined?
Loan typeConventional (incl. CONV-PMI), FHA, VA, Other (USDA / Farm / unknown)Never
Remaining term≤ 240 months (20 yrs), > 240 monthsNever
Original FICO≤ 750, > 750 (missing FICO is treated as ≤ 750)Never
Loan balance≤ $150K, $150–250K, $250–400K, > $400KYes, with neighboring bands
Rate spread0.25-point steps from −2% to +2%, plus "below −2%" and "above +2%"Yes, with neighboring bands

Every possible combination of these bands (1,728 in total) starts as a candidate group, so any future loan always has a home. Neighboring balance or spread bands are then combined when a band is too thin to give a reliable estimate (for example, fewer than about 1,500 loan observations), or when neighboring bands already prepay at nearly the same speed. A combination is skipped if it would make the resulting group noticeably less uniform. The combining rules are governed model settings and may be tuned at future reviews.

6Stage 2 — Assign cohorts to every funded loan

Every funded loan in Analytics_Rpt.md.B2BPricingHist is tagged with a cohort using its attributes at the pricing decision. The tag is stored in Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_revamp_cohorts.

6.1 Anchor date (CohortBaseDt)

ChannelAnchor dateWhy
CorrespondentLock date (LockDt). Fund date is used if no lock date is recorded.The rate, and therefore the refinance incentive, is set at lock, which is when the purchase is priced.
Co-IssueFund date (FundDt)No separate lock date is available.

6.2 What is captured at the anchor date

  • Loan type, remaining term, original FICO, loan balance and note rate, from the first servicing snapshot on or after the anchor month.
  • Market rate: the average of three 30-year mortgage rate series as of the anchor date: Freddie Mac PMMS, Optimal Blue and Mortgage News Daily.
  • Rate spread = note rate − market rate.

6.3 One cohort for the life of the loan

The cohort is stored on every monthly row of the loan as cohort_unique_ID (a code such as C033) and Rule_Bucket (the readable rule). A monthly check confirms that no loan ever carries more than one cohort. Balance and market rates change every month; the cohort does not.

7Stage 3 — Produce the B2B score

7.1 What goes in

  • Performance (score window): month-by-month balances, payoffs and recaptures for loans of any funding vintage, for the months selected (e.g. Oct-2025 → May-2026). Source: Analytics_Rpt.md.B2BPricingHist2, joined to each loan's cohort.
  • Funded volume (funding window): trailing 12 months of funded Correspondent and Co-Issue loans by default. Drives loan counts, funded amount, volume rank and weighted-average FICO / LTV / DTI / rate.
  • Excluded: reverse mortgages, transferred (XFER) or bulk-acquired loans, and loans in PR / VI / GU.

Actual CPR measures voluntary prepayment: full payoffs plus large curtailments (balance drops of $10K or more in a month).

7.2 CPRScore

Client CPR = 1 − ( Σ client ending balance ÷ Σ client beginning balance )12All of the client's cohort-assigned loan-months in the score window, pooled and annualized.
Peer CPR = 1 − ( Σ cohort ending balance ÷ Σ cohort beginning balance )12Summed over every cohort the client has loans in, using all clients' loans in those cohorts.
CPRScore = Client CPR ÷ Peer CPRFor the portfolio as a whole, client and peers are the same loans, so the portfolio CPRScore = 1.00.

7.3 RecapScore and TotalScore

RecapScore = Actual recapture rate ÷ Benchmark recapture rate   ·   TotalScore = RecapScore ÷ CPRScoreRecapture rate = balance of new Onity loans on payoffs ÷ payoff balance, for Purchase, Rate/Term Refinance and Cash-Out Refinance payoffs.

7.4 Special cases

SituationWhat the score does
Too little data: fewer than 20 loan-months in the score windowCPRScore is set to a neutral 1.0
Too few payoffs (fewer than 5) to measure recaptureRecapScore is set to a neutral 1.0
Eligible payoffs but nothing recapturedTotalScore = 0.5 ÷ CPRScore
No prepayment at all during the windowTotalScore is set relative to the portfolio score and rises with the amount of evidence: 1.0 × portfolio under 20 loan-months, then 1.25 × log10(loan-months) × portfolio

7.5 F / N / U tag

Portfolio score Z = TotalScore of the whole book  ·  Favorable if TotalScore ≥ 1.25 Z  ·  Unfavorable if TotalScore < 0.75 Z  ·  Neutral otherwise

8.1 B2B_revamp_cohorts: which cohort each loan belongs to

Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_revamp_cohorts
Key: LoanNum + AsofDt · One row per funded loan per month-end · History from Feb-2023 · 12.3M rows / 567K loans at the Sep-2026 rebuild, growing each month

How the table is kept up to date

  • Every month, after month-end data lands, a new month of rows is added for every funded loan.
  • New loans funded during the month get their cohort from their own lock or fund date attributes.
  • Existing loans carry the same cohort forward. Their monthly balances and rates are updated; their cohort is not.
  • If the cohort definitions are refreshed at a model review, every loan in history is re-tagged with the new set, so every month is compared on the same basis.

Example queries

-- A single loan: its cohort and anchor details
SELECT TOP 1 LoanNum, Channel, CohortBaseDtType, CohortBaseDt,
       LoanType_Base, RemainingTerm_Base, FICOOrig_Base, PrinBal_Base,
       IntRate_Base, Market_Rate_Base, cohort_unique_ID, Rule_Bucket
FROM   Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_revamp_cohorts
WHERE  LoanNum = '<LoanNum>'
ORDER BY AsofDt DESC;

-- A client's current book by cohort (the client name comes from B2BPricingHist)
SELECT c.cohort_unique_ID, c.Rule_Bucket,
       COUNT(*) AS Loans, SUM(c.PrinBal) AS UPB
FROM   Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_revamp_cohorts c
JOIN   Analytics_Rpt.md.B2BPricingHist h
       ON h.LoanNum = c.LoanNum AND h.AsofDt = c.AsofDt
WHERE  c.AsofDt = '2026-09-30'
  AND  h.Client = 'AMERICAN PACIFIC MORTGAGE CORPORATION'
GROUP BY c.cohort_unique_ID, c.Rule_Bucket
ORDER BY UPB DESC;

Why did a client score the way it did? Four steps

1
Pull the client's rows
Filter on Client. Sort by Client_BeginPrinBal so the cohorts holding most of its balance come first.
2
Read the headline
Client_ActualCPR vs Cohort_ActualCPR gives the CPRScore. Above 1 means faster than peers.
3
Find the drivers
In the large cohorts, compare Client_x_Cohort_ActualCPR with Cohort_x_Cohort_ActualCPR. Big gaps in big cohorts drive the score.
4
Re-derive it
Sum the client's balances and apply the CPR formula (query below). The result matches the published CPRScore.
-- Step 1 and 3: a client's cohort-level detail
SELECT Cohort_ID, Rule_bucket, Client_LoanCt,
       Client_BeginPrinBal,
       Client_BeginPrinBal / SUM(Client_BeginPrinBal) OVER () AS Share_of_Client_Balance,
       Client_x_Cohort_ActualCPR  AS Client_CPR_in_Cohort,
       Cohort_x_Cohort_ActualCPR  AS Peer_CPR_in_Cohort,
       Client_x_Cohort_ActualCPR - Cohort_x_Cohort_ActualCPR AS Gap_vs_Peers,
       Included_Flag, ExclusionReason
FROM   Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_CPRScore_Audit
WHERE  Client = 'AMERICAN PACIFIC MORTGAGE CORPORATION'
ORDER BY Client_BeginPrinBal DESC;

-- Step 4: re-derive the client's CPRScore from the cohort rows
SELECT Client,
       1 - POWER(SUM(Client_EndPrinBal) / SUM(Client_BeginPrinBal), 12) AS Client_CPR,
       1 - POWER(SUM(Cohort_EndPrinBal) / SUM(Cohort_BeginPrinBal), 12) AS Peer_CPR,
       (1 - POWER(SUM(Client_EndPrinBal) / SUM(Client_BeginPrinBal), 12))
     / NULLIF(1 - POWER(SUM(Cohort_EndPrinBal) / SUM(Cohort_BeginPrinBal), 12), 0) AS CPRScore_Derived,
       MAX(CPRScore) AS CPRScore_Published
FROM   Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_CPRScore_Audit
WHERE  Client = 'AMERICAN PACIFIC MORTGAGE CORPORATION'
GROUP BY Client;

-- Bonus: the client vs its peers by loan type
SELECT LEFT(Rule_bucket, CHARINDEX(' |', Rule_bucket) - 1) AS LoanType,
       SUM(Client_BeginPrinBal) AS Client_Begin_Bal,
       1 - POWER(SUM(Client_EndPrinBal) / SUM(Client_BeginPrinBal), 12) AS Client_CPR,
       1 - POWER(SUM(Cohort_EndPrinBal) / SUM(Cohort_BeginPrinBal), 12) AS Peer_CPR
FROM   Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_CPRScore_Audit
WHERE  Client = 'AMERICAN PACIFIC MORTGAGE CORPORATION'
GROUP BY LEFT(Rule_bucket, CHARINDEX(' |', Rule_bucket) - 1);
Why summing the cohort columns gives the benchmark
Each of a client's rows is a different cohort, so adding up Cohort_BeginPrinBal and Cohort_EndPrinBal over the client's rows pools the peers of every cohort the client participates in. That pooled run-off is the client's peer benchmark. Each cohort counts with its full peer balance, so the benchmark reflects how those peer groups performed in the market.
Prefer Excel?
The same rows are in the cprAuditLong sheet of the monthly workbook. Filter on the client and use =1-(SUM(Client_EndPrinBal)/SUM(Client_BeginPrinBal))^12 for the client CPR, and the same formula on the Cohort_ columns for the peer CPR. Divide one by the other to get the CPRScore. The cprAuditWide sheet shows the same information with one row per client and one column block per cohort.

For the client's RecapScore, TotalScore and tag, use B2B_ClientScore_final (below) or the rawTotal sheet.

9New methodology vs older methodology

Older: model benchmark

Client CPR ÷ balance-weighted ModelCPR produced by the Situs model (projected lifetime CPR).

Measures a client against a forecast. Any forecast bias for a loan type or rate environment passes straight into every client's score.

New: peer cohort benchmark

Client CPR ÷ actual CPR of all peer loans in the same fixed cohorts, over the same months.

Measures a client against loans like its own in the same market. Market-wide moves cancel out, and the portfolio scores 1.00.

12Frequently asked questions

What is a "peer cohort"?

A group of loans that shared the same loan type, remaining-term band, FICO band, balance group and rate-vs-market group when they were priced. There are 116 such groups. A client's loans are compared only with other lenders' loans in the same groups.

Does the score use a prepayment model?

Only to design the cohorts. The benchmark is the actual run-off of peer loans in the same months.

Why is the portfolio CPRScore exactly 1.00?

For the whole book, the client and the peers are the same loans. Each client's score therefore shows its position relative to the book, and a market-wide refinance wave does not push every client's score down at once.

Can a loan change cohort over time?

No. The cohort is set from attributes at lock (Correspondent) or funding (Co-Issue). It changes only if the whole cohort set is refreshed, and then all history is re-tagged.

Is lower or higher better?

CPRScore: lower is better (slower prepayment). RecapScore and TotalScore: higher is better. Favorable is TotalScore ≥ 1.25 × portfolio; Unfavorable is < 0.75 × portfolio.

Why did my client's tag change from the older score?

The client's own CPR did not change; the yardstick did. The Situs model under-predicted prepayment by different amounts for each loan type (most for VA). Clients heavy in under-predicted segments improve. Clients that prepay faster than their own look-alikes fall.

How do I check a client's score myself?

Query Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_CPRScore_Audit for the client and follow the four steps in Section 8.2, or filter the cprAuditLong sheet in Excel.

How do I find which cohort a specific loan is in?

Look up the loan in Analytics_Rpt.md.B2B_revamp_cohorts. cohort_unique_ID and Rule_Bucket are the same on every month for that loan.

Are the FICO / LTV / rate bands in the report the cohort bands?

No. Report bands (FICO 780+ / 740 / 700 …, LTV, DTI, note-rate and loan-balance buckets) are display breakdowns. Cohort bands are described in Section 5.3 and the Appendix.
